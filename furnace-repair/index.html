A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Malden, MA, the failures cluster at predictable points: a flame sensor coated in oxide that can no longer prove the flame, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Malden HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians watch the startup step by step and measure at each stage: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: the heat exchanger inspected, connections leak-tested, and draft confirmed, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Malden

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

The most serious furnace problem is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and the margin for error shrinks. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and replacing a good switch only hides the real fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
