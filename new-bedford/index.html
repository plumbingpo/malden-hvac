When Your System Fails in New Bedford

When your heat or air quits, how fast help arrives matters more than anything else. That's why we keep our schedule flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat as top priority.

These are the problems that come up most when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain overflowing into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Most of them come down to a part we already carry: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and common fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. That small step can save you from a much larger repair.

Keeping Your New Bedford HVAC System Running Longer

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.
